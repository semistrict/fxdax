# Reading and looking at a framing's parts on threads, one thread per CPU

**Tried:** 2026-11-13, TASK-336, main 487f443a8 (`extent_store::framing::read` on `on_threads`), replaced the same day.
**Idea:** a framing read its 32 MiB, zero-scanned, sampled and checksummed it on one thread, which on a host whose vCPUs keep its CPUs busy gets one thread's share of them: on threads, each part read, looked at and checksummed where it is, the framing gets as many shares as it has threads, as its compressing already did.
**Result:** with part buffers and compressors kept (`framing::KEPT`), store-bench framing on ubu1 halved under 28 spinning threads either way. But on ubu1's sandboxes short mode, interleaved against main before TASK-336 (fxjob --bench, two rounds each order): builders 84.5-120 s against 79.7-88 s, the run's I/O stall (PSI some) 10-35 s against 7-14 s, durable lag no better and in one round far worse (29 s max). The same with reading on the framer's thread (ABBA, short9v/9b/10b/10v): builders 75.6-80.8 s against 80.6-84.6 s, I/O stall 5.7-9.9 s against 8.9-13.3 s, slow framings 28-36 against 56-62 a host, the slowest 201-288 ms against 244-423 ms. Long mode on 487f443a8 failed 5 framings past 1 s, all waiting on the disk.
**Why it's dead:** the reading was slow for its page faults, not for want of threads; more threads per framing preempt the vCPUs in bursts, and the VMs and the disk pay for it, for no framing time the kept buffers don't already win back.
**Reopen if:** framings must read data not in the page cache, where the read is the disk's, or the host's CPUs are not the VMs'.
